## Q13
- **Category:** jurisdiction_comparison
- **Topic:** overtime
- **Phrasing:** casual
- **Question:** We have staff in Toronto and Vancouver who'd rather bank their overtime and take time off later. Can we do that in both provinces, and do the rules differ?

**Ontario** (s. 22)
- Answer: Yes, if the employer and employee agree. The employee receives 1.5 hours of paid time off for each overtime hour, taken within 3 months of the work week in which it was earned (or within 12 months with the employee's agreement). If employment ends before the time off is taken, overtime pay is owed.
- Key facts:
  - Requires agreement between employer and employee
  - 1.5 hours of paid time off per overtime hour
  - Taken within 3 months, or within 12 months with the employee's agreement
  - Paid out as overtime pay if employment ends first

**British Columbia** (s. 42)
- Answer: Yes. At the employee's written request, the employer may set up a time bank and credit overtime wages to it at overtime rates. The employee can ask at any time to be paid out, to use the credit for paid time off at an agreed time, or to close the bank. The employer may close it with one month's written notice and must settle it within 6 months. The balance must be paid on termination or when the employee asks to close it.
- Key facts:
  - Set up at the employee's written request
  - Overtime credited at overtime rates
  - Employee may request payout, time off, or closing at any time
  - Employer may close with 1 month's written notice and settle within 6 months
  - Balance paid on termination or on the employee's request to close

**Comparison:** Different: both allow banking overtime, but Ontario uses an agreement with a 3- or 12-month deadline to take the time off, while BC uses a time bank set up at the employee's written request, with no use-by deadline stated while it is open.

**Abstain:** none

- Key facts:
  - Employer must allow vacation in periods of one or more weeks
  - Periods shorter than a week are not addressed in s. 57

**Comparison:** Similar: both are built around whole weeks. Ontario allows shorter periods by written request plus employer agreement; BC requires the employer to allow periods of one week or more.
